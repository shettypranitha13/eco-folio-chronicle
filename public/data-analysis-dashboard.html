      Findings & recommendations

      Based on the full 850-record dataset.

      Key insights

      • Streams

        Plastic, organic and e-waste each account for roughly a fifth of daily tonnage — there is no single dominant waste stream to target; construction and hazardous waste are close behind.

      • Campaigns

        Awareness campaign counts show almost no linear relationship with recycling rates (r ≈ -0.02), suggesting existing campaigns are either saturated or not well targeted.

      • Disposal

        Landfill and incineration sites post the highest average recycling rates, implying that sorting infrastructure is concentrated at large facilities rather than distributed at the source.

      • Efficiency

        Municipal efficiency scores are essentially uncorrelated with recycling rate (r ≈ -0.03) — a highly-rated municipality is no more likely to recycle well than a low-rated one, in this data.

      • Density

        Denser cities are not charged more per ton (r ≈ -0.03) and tend to run slightly smaller landfills (r ≈ -0.25), the opposite of an economies-of-scale pattern.

      Recommendations

      • 01

        Shift from campaign volume to targeted segregation education for plastic and organic waste specifically, where the tonnage is largest.

      • 02

        Decentralize composting infrastructure so organic waste is processed closer to source, instead of routing recyclables through landfill-adjacent sorting.

      • 03

        Extend e-waste-specific collection and sorting to the lowest-performing cities identified above, rather than applying a uniform national program.

      • 04

        Audit budget allocation independent of density or efficiency score, since neither currently predicts cost or recycling outcomes in this data.
